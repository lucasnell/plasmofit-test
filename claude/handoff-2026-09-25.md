## What each one asks, and how to read it

### Option 1 (28957): does `b_shape` ~ 65 repair the trend misfit?

`_scripts/wockner-ppc-trend.R`. The panel showed that widening `b_shape`'s
prior gains 24.8 elpd and shortens `cycle_length` by 0.47-1.29 h, but sends
`b_shape` to 65.4 with a posterior sd of 50. Two readings:

- **(a)** the data want a sharp stage window and the default prior suppressed it
- **(b)** the model is absorbing structural lack of fit into the one nuisance
  parameter now free to run

(b) has independent support: real series rise and fall more steeply than the
fitted trajectories, observed median within-series range **2.563** log10
units against 1.95-2.21 simulated. A sharper window is exactly what lets the
model chase a steeper decline.

**Read `ppp` in the output table.** It is `P(T(y_rep) >= T(y_obs))` over 200
draws; ~0.5 means the fit reproduces the statistic, near 0 means it generates
series flatter than the real ones. Compare `no_pool` against
`np_wide_bshape`, and `np_wide_total0` against `np_wide_both`.

**The asymmetry matters.** A gap that *survives* under `b_shape` ~ 65 rules
out (a) as a complete account. A gap that *closes* is consistent with both --
a model absorbing lack of fit also fits better. So a closed gap does not
settle it, and option 2 is what settles it.

### Option 2 (28941): can this design recover `b_shape` = 65 at all?

Simulates from `pl_wide_both`, whose truth has `b_shape` ~ 65, and fits under
the same widened priors (arm `wide_nuis` matches `sd_log_b_shape = 1.5`,
`sd_log10_total0 = 1` exactly). If the fit returns ~15, the real-data 65 is
not a measurement and reading (b) gains. If it returns ~65, the design can
see a sharp window when one is there and the 65 has to be taken seriously.

**The truth's `cycle_length` is 43.7361 h here, not the 45.012 h every other
simulation in this project uses.** Read bias against what the job prints.

### Option 3 (28940): make the cycle-length budget mean something

Every structural effect the budget separates (`b_shape` prior ~0.50 h,
hierarchy ~0.25 h, cycle-length prior ~0.15 h) is smaller than the
replicate-to-replicate spread at n=3 (+0.93 vs +1.97 h on the same design,
differing only in noise). Replicates 4-5 of `default`, `wide`, `no_hier`,
`wide_bshape`, `wide_total0`, `wide_nuis`, plus **`no_hier-rep1`, which was
never run** -- `findings.md`'s hierarchy table only ever had rep2 and rep3.

The panel also found the two nuisance priors **super-additive on
`cycle_length`** (jointly -1.1 h against -0.27 h one-at-a-time), so the
one-at-a-time budget is the wrong decomposition. Read `wide_nuis` as the
joint effect rather than summing `wide_bshape` and `wide_total0`.

## Reading it back: `_scripts/wockner-schedule-sim-analyze.R`

Globs `wock-schedsim-RES-*.rds`, so both new batches are picked up with no
argument. It was **edited on 2026-09-25** and checked against the 23 RES
files that predate this run: it runs clean and reproduces the thread 2
recovery table unchanged.

**What changed and why it mattered.** Option 2's replicates record
`truth_source = "mean"` exactly like every mean-vector replicate, but their
truth is a different fit with a different `cycle_length`. Grouping on
`truth_source` alone would have pooled them and averaged two truths. `src` is
now `<source>@<truth fit>` -- `mean@pooled_cl` for everything that existed
before, `mean@pl_wide_both` for option 2 -- with a guard that errors if
replicates in one group disagree on `true_cl`. **If that guard fires, do not
work around it**; it means two truths are about to be averaged.

## Caveats to carry into the write-up

- **Replicates 4-5 were fitted against a recompiled package.** The inoculum
  anchor added `delta_total0`/`sigma_total0` declarations to all four Stan
  programs since rep1-3 were run. With the anchor off they are `array[0]` and
  contribute nothing, so the model is mathematically identical -- but per
  `gotchas.md` two fits are never bit-identical across a recompile. For
  pooling across noise replicates this is immaterial, since replicates differ
  by noise seed anyway. Do not use rep4-5 in any bit-exact regression test.
- **Do not mix the two truths.** 28940 is 45.012 h, 28941 is 43.7361 h.
- `no_hier` has no between-trial `cycle_length` variation, so its correlation
  columns are `NA` by construction, not by failure.
- The real-data prior comparisons remain n=1 by construction. More simulation
  replicates do nothing for that.

## Two known traps left in place on purpose

- **The package default `sd_log10_total0 = 0.25` is still there**
  (`plasmofit/R/archer-fit.R:106`). It implies a 647% establishment fraction
  and costs 104.7 elpd, and the decision on 2026-09-25 was to leave the
  package alone and pin the priors in this repo's scripts instead.
  `wockner-schedule-sim.R` and `wockner-ppc-trend.R` now write the nuisance
  priors out explicitly in their `archer_stan_data()` calls.
- **`wockner-fit.R` is NOT pinned.** It still inherits the package defaults,
  so a future change to them would silently redefine what `no_pool` means and
  break the eight-fit panel's baseline. Pinning it was not done because
  `np_anchor` passes `inoc_size`, which replaces the `log10_total0` prior,
  and whether `archer_stan_data()` accepts both together is untested. Do that
  test before pinning.

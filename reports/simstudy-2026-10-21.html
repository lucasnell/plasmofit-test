Simulation study of cycle-length bias

Results as they stand, 2026-10-21. All 240 planned fits ran or were cancelled by decision; no second stage. Design and reading rule fixed before any fit ran (_scripts/simstudy.R header).

Bottom line
  1. The fitted n_c moves the cycle length. On identical simulated data, fitting n_c = 384 instead of 192 gives a shorter cycle_length in 12 of 12 paired replicates: −0.10 to −1.49 h, mean −0.55 h, whichever n_c generated the data.
  2. Bias against the truth is within about ±1.2 h in the 9 cells that can be read, positive in 8 of 9 (−0.60 to +1.19 h). Replicate ranges are 1–2 h wide, so the cells cannot be told apart.
  3. Fixing b_shape does not work at fit n_c = 384, even at its true value: 1 of 25 fits converges. The pin-b_shape strategy settled at n_c = 96 does not carry over.
  4. Most of the design is unreadable: 62 of 205 fits pass the convergence gate, and 39 of 48 cells have fewer than 3 passing replicates. That includes every cell that generates at 384 and fits at 192, so the bias from under-specifying n_c is not measured, nor how bias depends on the true b_shape or on how it is fitted.

Paired contrasts

Each dot is one replicate: the difference in 13-trial mean posterior cycle_length, hours, between two fits of the same simulated dataset, both passing the gate. Dashed line: mean over dots.

Fit n_c 384 − fit n_c 192 (12 pairs). At generate 192 this over-specifies n_c; at generate 384 it is the correct fit minus the under-specified one.

All 12 negative, so a sign test gives two-sided p = 0.0005. The caveat is selection: only pairs in which both fits passed are shown, and passing may not be independent of the estimate.

b_shape estimated − fixed at truth (9 pairs), same fit n_c.

6 of 9 positive, −0.18 to +1.36 h. The direction matches the earlier attribution of simulated bias to estimating b_shape (2026-10-06, at n_c 96), but nine pairs cannot confirm it.

What this means for the analysis

  • A reported cycle length must state its n_c. On simulated data, moving the fit from 192 to 384 moves it by about half an hour on identical data, comparable to the bias itself.
  • Which n_c is less biased is not answered. The cells that would show the cost of under-specifying (generate 384, fit 192) are all unreadable, and the readable diagonal cells overlap.
  • At n_c = 384, b_shape has to be estimated. Fixing it fails even at the truth, so the failures of pinned real-data fits at 384 are what the model does when it is correctly specified, not a sign of misfit to the real data.
  • No bias correction can be read from this study. Biases of −0.6 to +1.2 h, with replicate ranges of 1–2 h, do not pin down a correction for the real-data estimates (41.2–41.8 h).

Not measured, and caveats

  • Bias from under-specifying n_c; dependence of bias on the true b_shape; the cost of estimating rather than fixing b_shape.
  • Truth A comes from an n_c = 96 fit, which is known to be the wrong n_c; its parameters are used only as a plausible generating point.
  • 500 draws per chain, half the production length, chosen to keep 4,000 total draws over 8 chains. A second stage with 1,000 draws was costed (about 4–7 days at the half-node budget) and not run.
  • Paired contrasts include only pairs where both fits passed.

=== 1. the Poisson convolution reproduces mat_exp_series ===
Cells: max over the 7 observation times of |convolution - mat_exp_series|
/ mat_exp_series, at cycle_length 45, b_shape 400, R 8, mu 0. This is a
reimplementation from the ODE's structure, not a refactor, so agreement
at 1e-12 is an independent check of both.

  n_c =  96 : max rel err = 4.145e-12
  n_c = 192 : max rel err = 1.012e-12
  n_c = 384 : max rel err = 8.306e-13

=== 2. a continuous kernel is NOT a reparameterisation of the chain ===
Cells: max over the 7 times of |log10(IPM) - log10(chain)|, in log10
units, at mesh M = n_c with the kernel's variance matched to the chain's.
The only difference is the kernel: the chain's stage at time t is exactly
POISSON, a lattice distribution, while both IPM kernels are continuous.
For scale, the residual sd of these ML fits is about 0.48 log10 units.

  n_c =  96 : gaussian 0.0135 | gamma 0.0120
  n_c = 192 : gaussian 0.0331 | gamma 0.0310
  n_c = 384 : gaussian 0.0623 | gamma 0.0595

The gamma keeps the chain's right skew and the gaussian does not, yet
they differ by under 0.003 log10 units, so SKEW IS NOT THE GAP. The gap
grows with n_c, which is the opposite of the kernel-shape argument, and
the next table says why.

=== 3. the gap is one point, and it is the deepest trough ===
Cells: the chain's predicted circulating parasitaemia and the gamma IPM's,
per observation time, at n_c = 384; dlog10 is log10(IPM) - log10(chain).
The observable spans four orders of magnitude across the cycle because
sequestration hides ~60% of it, and at a trough the value is set by the
TAIL of the age distribution -- exactly where a lattice Poisson and a
continuous gamma differ.

    t cycles      chain        ipm      dlog10

trough / peak ratio in the chain: 7e-06. All of the discrepancy is at
t = 72 h; the other six times agree to 0.006 log10 units or better.
Troughs are also where low-end censoring is an open question (TODO), so
this is the one place the kernel choice and a known data issue coincide.

=== 4. the decoupling, which is the whole point ===
Cells: max |log10 difference| from the chain at n_c = 384, the dispersion
the data prefer. Row 1 is the chain forced onto a coarse mesh, which also
coarsens its dispersion because it has only one knob. The IPM rows use
the SAME coarse mesh with the fine dispersion dialled in.

  chain, n_c = 96 (mesh and rate locked together) : 1.5888
  IPM gamma, M =  96, n_eff = 384                 : 0.0638
  IPM gamma, M = 192, n_eff = 384                 : 0.0605

A factor of about 25 in log10 units. Mesh and rate are separated.

=== 5. dispersion is continuous for the IPM and quantised for the chain ===
Cells: sigma_c is the sd in hours of a parasite's age advance after one
cycle; the last column is the stage distribution's sd at the final
observation. The chain reaches only these rows, at cubic cost to move
between them; the IPM reaches any value at a fixed mesh and fixed cost.

  n_c sigma_c_h sd_at_4.8_cycles_h
1  96     4.593             10.062
2 192     3.248              7.115
3 384     2.296              5.031
4 768     1.624              3.558

=== 6. cost ===
Cells: seconds for one 7-point trajectory, median of 5 runs.
mat_exp_series exponentiates a 2*n_c square matrix, which is cubic; the
convolutions are one FFT pair per time point.

  n_c =  96 : mat_exp  0.0150 | poisson-conv  0.0060 | ipm-gamma  0.0070 | speedup     2x
  n_c = 192 : mat_exp  0.1540 | poisson-conv  0.0090 | ipm-gamma  0.0110 | speedup    14x
  n_c = 384 : mat_exp  1.1790 | poisson-conv  0.0280 | ipm-gamma  0.0310 | speedup    38x

The speedup is available WITHOUT any rewrite: the Poisson convolution is
the same model as mat_exp_series, agreeing to 1e-12, and it is the column
to port if cost is the motive rather than decoupling.
